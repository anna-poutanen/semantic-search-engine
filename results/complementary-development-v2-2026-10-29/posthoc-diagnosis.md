# Complementary context v2: post hoc gain/loss diagnosis

This is explanatory analysis of already-saved outcomes, written after the canonical independent audit passed all 393 candidate rows and 786 controls. The frozen **quality gate failed**. This diagnostic is outside that audit and does not change the rule, labels, historical results, qualification gate or product default. No new variant, hypothetical gate, native run, model inference, study-data retrieval or reserved-corpus access occurred.

Across 201 distinct observed dataset/query units, v2 added one complete multi-file behavior over coverage (Jinja bytecode rejection/recompilation) and lost one (TanStack mutation scope continuation). It gained two group occurrences and lost two versus coverage, with no single-file-first-hit losses. There were 129 admitted plans, 119 accepted guards, 10 guard fallbacks and 264 self references. No groups or complete behaviors were lost versus legacy. Starlette exception registration/dispatch and Zod metadata completion were already coverage gains; they are not new v2 completions.

## Jinja: a retrieved implementation was admitted successfully

For `jinja_bytecode_reject_and_recompile`, `BaseLoader.load:107–149` already existed at raw rank **16**, in the portfolio and original canonical pool. Both controls omitted it from their eight-row allocation. V2 admitted it, and the full window reached final rank 7 with the entire required lines **130–145** and exact qualified symbol/evidence hash. `Bucket.load_bytecode` remained at rank 1, completing both required groups. Neither character nor token clipping was involved: the final eight rows used 4,145 source characters and 1,845 tokens.

The second admission, `Undefined.__init__` at raw rank 41, increased lexical support but supplied neither required group. The actual guard accepted the observed proposal. Applying the unchanged independent `source_covers` helper to every actual coverage row shows **6 of 8** retained: the proposal replaced `FileSystemBytecodeCache.load_bytecode:264–277` and `dump_bytecode:279–315`. Thus this successful gold-group allocation preserves the protected subset, not all original coverage source.

## TanStack: a backfilled implementation lost its excerpt slot

For dense `tanstack_mutation_scope_serial_continuation`, the full `Mutation.execute:173–313` window was already at raw rank **11**. It remained in the two-window portfolio, original canonical pool and returned selected/backfill pool. The coverage control appended it as its eighth row, covering group 1 (**184–203**, retryer setup and permission) and group 3 (**310–312**, the final continuation path). V2 did not admit it or include it in proposed eligible rows.

Instead, v2 admitted `MutationObserver.reset:119–126` at raw rank **45** and evicted the baseline `QueryClient.resumePausedMutations` row. After three nested rows were coalesced, unchanged backfill immediately re-added that evicted QueryClient row, then `Mutation.continue` and `MutationCache.add`. The eight-excerpt limit was reached before `Mutation.execute`, which remained at position 11 in the backfill pool. This is **backfill-slot displacement**, not a retrieval miss, portfolio discard or clipped window. All eight final rows were untruncated and used only **2,561 characters / 1,518 tokens** (coverage used 6,051 / 2,407).

The guard accepted correctly according to its frozen safeguards. `Mutation.execute` was not among the protected reference IDs, protected exact definitions or overlapping protected windows. Its two required groups therefore had no source-floor protection. The reset comment says “another mutate call will yield a new mutation”; `another` has document frequency **1**, while `current` also matches the query aspect. The observed second-aspect support increased from **2556/51661** to **11250/51661**. The actual `Mutation.execute` window only scored **1015/51661** on that aspect. Preserved or improved lexical maxima therefore coexisted with loss of the required execution/finalization behavior.

The unchanged independent `source_covers` check certifies **7 of 8** actual coverage rows retained; the only uncovered reference row is `Mutation.execute:173–313`. All protected rows remained covered. The proposal and final response are the exact same accepted source payload. Original window hashes, literal physical-line source, saved provenance and raw or dependency-owner scalars remain truthful.

## Design implication and evidence

Joint allocation must account for what the **actual coverage backfill** contributes, and rare literal comment/identifier matches cannot be treated as behavioral evidence. Both the successful Jinja proposal and the failing TanStack proposal replace existing unprotected coverage source under the eight-row cap. This analysis certifies those observed properties only; it tests no all-source guard, hypothetical gate or new remedy. Failed v2 remains research, and the production coverage default remains unchanged.

[The JSON diagnostic](posthoc-diagnosis.json) binds the frozen plan, run, raw ledger, controls, canonical audit and old literal-helper hashes. It retains every OR alternative and strict path, qualified-symbol, complete physical-interval and evidence-SHA stage check; raw/portfolio/selection/allocation/packed/reference/proposal/final coverage; exact original control references; all-source preservation witnesses; scalar/source checks; and actual guard/backfill receipts. Jinja keeps its original historical scoring flag while the diagnostic additionally verifies its exact qualified symbol and evidence bytes. No evidence labels or original results were rewritten.
